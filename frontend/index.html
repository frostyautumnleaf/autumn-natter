Chatting
Fold the thoughts as they come in The head of a thinking block stands on its own while the model thinks, and the words of it stay out of the way. Turn it off to read the thoughts as they arrive. A click on the head of one block opens or folds that block.
Fold a command as it runs The head of a tool block stands on its own while its command runs. A click on the head of one block opens or folds that block.
Colours of a code block The words of a block of code take the colour of their kind: a comment, a word of the language, a word in quotes, a number. Turn it off to read a block in one colour.
The chapters of a chat The model may mark where a new part of the chat begins. The marks stand on a line at the left of the chat, and a click on one of them takes you there. Turn this off to hide the line, and the model is no longer offered the marks.
Commands in one answer How many terminal commands the model may run inside one answer. -1 leaves no limit, so the model may run a command as often as it wants. A mark of a chapter is not a command and eats none of this room.
runs, -1 for no limit
Steps in one answer How many turns of the model one answer may take. A turn is asked again after every command, so a long talk with many parts needs room here. -1 leaves no limit, and the answer then ends only when the model stops by itself.
turns, -1 for no limit
A paste of words becomes a file When a paste into the chat box holds more characters than this, the words turn into a file hung on the message instead of standing in the box. The least is 100.
characters
Width of the chat How wide the column of the chat may be. 780 pixels is the width the chat always had, and it is the least. The chat never grows past 80 per cent of the width of the window, and it keeps clear of the line of the chapters, the line of the summarisations and the arrows of the chat.
pixels
Compaction
Compaction How the room of the talk is made again when it runs out. The handoff summary asks the model, without your being asked anything, for a summary of the talk when the room nears its end: the words before that summary are then left out of what goes back to the model, so a chat may go on for ever. The first message you sent is always kept, and the summary rides after it. Nothing leaves the chat alone until the room is gone.
Start the summarisation Either when the talk holds so great a share of the room of the model, or when the room left over has fallen under a number of tokens.
The share of the room The talk is summarised when it holds this share of the room the model was given.
per cent
The room to leave over The talk is summarised when the room left over is this small or smaller, so the next answer has room to be written.
tokens
The handoff prompt What the model is told to write when the summary is asked for. Leave it empty and the prompt of the program is used again. No longer than 4096 characters.
The short handoff prompt The first summary is given all of the room that is left and may be as long as it likes. When it fills that room and stops in the middle of itself, the model is asked again with these words, which lean towards a very short summary. Leave it empty and the prompt of the program is used again. No longer than 4096 characters.
The line of the summarisations A vertical line at the right of the chat, with one node for every time the talk was made short. A node says which summarisation it is and shows a little of it; a click takes you there.
